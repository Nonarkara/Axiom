searching through disconnected overlays. Preserve the full-bleed Leaflet map,

## Design Decision

Read Palette's `DESIGN-FIELD-GUIDE.md` and `styles.css` in the sibling codebase.
Borrowed its content-led spacing, shared edges and stable control targets, not
its theme or application structure. Two arrangements were considered:

- Retain a viewport-height composition with independent absolute overlays.
  Rejected because the controls, heading, brief and stats compete for space.
- Use a content-sized grid: control row, proposition alongside theatre brief,
  then city nodes alongside stats. Selected because related elements share
  alignment and locale-driven height changes remain in normal layout.

Phone layout preserves the brief and horizontally scrollable city controls.
Actions use compact 44px targets; redundant static map instructions are omitted
on phones. Headline phrase measurement reserves one stable row across rotation.
Resize observers keep Leaflet and canvas dimensions aligned with the grid.

Integrated the concurrently merged editorial edition rather than removing its
source. Its stylesheet is retained in a named fallback cascade layer; the current
hero system has explicit precedence. Source attribution and reduced-motion
behavior from that edition remain active. This avoids competing 900px minimum
height, split-map and absolute-position rules in the released layout.
Sticky-header anchor offsets and an earlier nav-collapse breakpoint prevent
return-to-top links or narrow desktop menus from obscuring the hero controls.

## Evidence And Limits

Desktop browser: 1280px-wide hero ends around 661px including masthead, so the
next section is visible in a 720px viewport. Map tiles, HUD and controls remain
visible. Seven languages were exercised at an actual 390px CSS viewport; no
hero action overflow was observed. A pre-existing code-locale overflow in
metadata/legal rows was identified and addressed with wrapping and phone stacks.
Chrome responsive testing was restored after a browser restart. Final checks
cover actual CSS widths of 320, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 pixels with no page
overflow; all seven locales were exercised again at 390px. The in-app browser's
viewport override did not change its effective dimensions, so responsive evidence
comes from Chrome. No uninstructed human walkthrough or exhaustive device
testing is claimed.
