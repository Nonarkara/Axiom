## Found, changed, verified

### Part one — the streamline

The hero is the operating table, so everything around it is an instrument at its
edge. Five of these instruments were sized by the grid rather than by their use.

| Element | Width | What failed | Cause | Repair |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `.sat-signal-label` | 390px | every context pin named its place at once: **12+ overlapping caption pairs** on a 390px map | the caption rule was global, so a rule that earned its place on a desktop map fired on a phone too | the rule now belongs to the wide layout (≥901px); the active, hovered and focused pin still name their place at every width |
| `.map-mode` | 1440px | a **684px** black slab, 80% empty | it sat in the 684px `mode` track and a grid item stretches to its track | `justify-self: start` — **684px → 133px** |
| `.sat-hud` | 1440px | a **480px** panel for a 164px of readout | `min-width: 280px` plus grid stretch | `min-width: 0; justify-self: end` — **480px → 164px**, docked to the right edge |
| `.sat-hud` | 390px | the HUD was **two bands, 65px** tall | the first grid column was a fixed 132px track, which wrapped "AUTO TOUR" onto two lines | the HUD rows run side by side and the signals line is hidden on a phone, the same call the file already makes for the tiles — **65px → 44px**, level with the 40px tour bar |
| `.hero-node-strip-head` | 1440px | a 10px caption and a 320px field **250px apart** across a void | `space-between` put the two ends of one group at the two ends of the row | the group starts together: `flex-start` and a wrap |

Two more changes in the same pass:

- **The console's four views are one instrument, not four boxes.** The per-cell
  `border-right` and its `:last-child` rule are gone; the row's own hairline
  separates it from the panel and the filled cell says which view is open.
- **The scroll cue was removed, not repaired.** It was a 1px × 32px hairline at
  the viewport's horizontal centre with no arrowhead and no label; at 1024px it
  landed in the 11px gutter between the copy column and the console with nothing
  around it, and it was already hidden below 700px. It is one line of markup to
  restore if it is ever wanted.
- The coverage rectangle was recoloured `#d4b96e` → `#e2b540`, the house ocher.

### Part two — the stretch massacre

ANTI-SLOP rule 9: *"No small thing is wider than 2× what its content needs. Stat
tiles go in a ruled strip sized to their text, never equal grid tracks."* A grid
item stretches to its track by default, and a fractional track is not a reason
for anything to be that wide. Four places were doing it.

| Element | Before | What it cost | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| `.hero-stat` | three equal `1fr` tracks of a 684px column | **"5" — 12px of text — in a 208px box, 17×** its own width; 139px of gap between the marks because the grid divided the column rather than the reading | the tiles take their text's width: **80px**; the marks sit 12px apart |
| `.probono-stack__chip` | two equal tracks | **"D1" — 12px of text — in a 167px cell at 390px (12.9×) and a 108px cell at 1440px (8×)** | a wrapping shelf of chips, each as wide as its name; **15 chips over the 2× limit → 0** |
| `.stack__items` | a `minmax(118px, max-content)` track | the 118px floor made every short name fill it: **"Git" — 15px of text — in a 118px box, 6.9×** | a wrapping shelf of chips, each as wide as its name; **25+ over the limit → 0** at every width |
| `.stack__head` | the role pushed to the far right of a 1018px track | the gloss that explains a layer landed **under the count, 900px from the name it describes** | the name and the gloss are one phrase and travel together (**900px → 12px**); the count keeps the right-hand edge, because nine counts in one column is worth aligning |

**The page is measurably shorter for it.** 117px shorter at 390 and 39px shorter
at 1440 — the space the tiles gave back went to the map, which is what is behind
them.

### Part three — one number, one meaning

The hero's duplicate 71 was resolved in commit `47abe3a` by giving the two figures
different meanings: the hero counted the catalogue (71) and the console counted
the live systems (62). The shape was right and one number was wrong, and the
page came to hold **two live counts that did not agree**.

| Figure | Before | What a reader could check against it | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| `.hero-console__metric-val` | 62 | the catalogue further down the page labels **60** cells "Live" | **60** |
| `.dot` — §4.9's live indicator | 62 | two of them sat on cells labelled "Preview" and "Coming soon" | **60**: one on every live cell, none on any other |
| `#evidenceLive` — the status strip | 60 | — | 60, and it now agrees with the console |

The defect had two halves and both had to go.

**The count was derived from a decoration.** The console counted `.dot`
elements. The status strip further down the page already counted the cells the
page labels Live, and had always been right. The page showed both numbers, one
of which was wrong. The console now counts the *labels*, so the two figures are
the same number by construction rather than by coincidence — and if a dot is
ever put back on a preview cell, the count stays honest.

**The indicator lied.** The blinking amber dot is §4.9's live indicator, and the
house rule is exact: 60 live cells wear it, the other 11 do not — Preview,
Coming soon, In Development and Sign-in required all appear without a dot.
`simcity` (Preview) and `rag` (Coming soon) wore it anyway. A blinking dot next
to "Coming soon" tells the reader two contradictory things at once, and no count
can repair a signal that contradicts its own label. Both dots were removed.

Measured in a browser at 1440px after the repair: 71 cells · 60 dots · 60 cells
labelled Live · **0 dots on a cell that is not live** · 0 live cells missing a
dot · console reads **60** · status strip reads **60** · hero reads **71**.

### Three findings that are recorded, not removed

The repairs above were re-measured by the gate and it found three new decisions
that it will not let pass unexplained. They are written down in
`design/spatial-baseline.json` under `reasons`, and the reasoning is worth
stating here because two of them are the honest price of the other two.

| Decision | Why it is allowed to exist |
|---|---|
| `wrap div.probono-stack` | The shelf lists the technologies a pro bono project was built on — four names in a 239px card. The line falls where the names run out of room. A technology name is not an option, an action or a control, so nothing the reader is hunting for is stranded on the last line. The layout before this was a two-column grid whose track every chip stretched into. A lone final name is the end of a list; a chip inflated to twelve times its name is a lie about the name. |
| `wrap div.stack__items` | The same argument for the full 114-name stack in nine layers. The alternative that fills every line — a fixed 118px track that each chip stretched into — made "Git" sit in a box nearly seven times its own width, and turned a list of names into a table of identical boxes. |
| `wrap div.stack__head` | Where the head is 288px the three parts need 298px, and the count takes a second line rather than the layer's name being broken in half. It is a figure, not an action, and it stays in the same right-hand column as every other layer's count. |

**A repair that trades one measured defect for another is not a repair.** The
stretch was removed and the orphan that appeared in its place is named, measured
and argued for, rather than left for the next agent to find.

## Remaining

### Observed and left alone — the hero's 71 is a catalogue count wearing a production label

The hero stat reads `71 Systems in Production`. 71 is the catalogue — every card
in the grid, counted from the DOM so it cannot drift. Of those 71, the page
labels **60 Live**, 6 Preview, 2 Coming soon, 1 In Development and 2 Sign-in
required, so three are explicitly not in production, and the console beside it
already says `60 Live Systems in Production`.

**Why it is not a massacre target.** The label is the pitch; MSH §5.6 does not
override the owner's judgement about copy, and the catalogue's own badges
answer the question honestly for anyone who scrolls. The fix, if he wants it,
is one key in seven locales — `hero.statSystems` in `app.js` and
`i18n-regional.js` — to "Systems in Catalogue" or "Systems Shipped". **No figure
would change.** **This one is Dr Non's to make.** It is recorded here rather
than silently changed.

### Observed — two figures in the console are hand-kept, not derived

`8 Critical Domains Served` and `0 Slide Decks / Mockups` are written into the
markup. The zero reads as a deliberate position — this practice ships systems,
not decks — and the eight has nothing on the page to be checked against.
Neither is counted from anything, so neither can be shown to have drifted, and
neither can be shown not to. Recorded, not changed: inventing a counter for
them would be a bigger claim than the two figures make.

### Observed and left alone — the map's subject sits partly behind the console

At 1440 the coverage rectangle and the "CHIANG MAI" label graze the console's
left edge. Correcting it means changing the AUTO TOUR's view maths, which is an
earned feature with real regression risk, and the console is a panel docked at
the edge of the operating table by design. Recorded, not changed.

## Method

`.qa/msh-evidence.mjs` measures the rendered interface and writes the
screenshots; the spatial gate measures the geometry it cannot see by eye;
`.qa/count-check.mjs` asserts that every figure on the page agrees with what the
page can be counted for. All three are repeatable, and all three are in the
repository so the next agent can argue with the numbers instead of with a
memory of them.

```bash
node .qa/msh-evidence.mjs                 # both surfaces, live
node .qa/msh-evidence.mjs --local         # against the local servers
node .qa/count-check.mjs                  # the live catalogue's figures
node .qa/count-check.mjs http://127.0.0.1:5312/
node scripts/spatial/spatial-check.mjs --base http://127.0.0.1:5312/ --routes "/,/admin/"
node .qa/spatial-sweep.mjs --base http://127.0.0.1:5311/ --routes "$(cat .shots/rr-routes.txt)" --chunk 4 --baseline design/spatial-baseline.json
```

**A gate that has never failed proves nothing.** The count check was written
against the defect, and it was run against the deployment *before* the repair:
it failed with three problems — two dots on cells that are not live, a console
that said 62 where the page labels 60, and the console disagreeing with the
strip below it. It then passed against the local repair. A check that has only
ever printed PASS has not been shown to be able to fail.

**A note for the next agent, paid for in an hour.** A baseline `--update`
rewrites the file from the routes measured in that run: sweep 2 of 73 and the
other 71 vanish, silently, and the gate still passes on the 2. Read the diff and
compare the route count after every update, and re-sweep what is missing before
trusting the result.
